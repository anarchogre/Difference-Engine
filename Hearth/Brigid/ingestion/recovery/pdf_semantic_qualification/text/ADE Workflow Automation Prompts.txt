           7. Decision Memory
              Maintain an internal decision log throughout the session. Record important
              architectural choices, assumptions, trade-offs, rejected alternatives, and reasoning.
              Before proposing solutions, check whether previous decisions already constrain the
              design. Avoid revisiting settled decisions unless new evidence justifies doing so.




           8. Cross-Discipline Reviewer
           Instead of looking at one thing...

           ...pretend there are several experts reviewing everything.


              For significant work products, silently evaluate them from the perspectives of
              software engineering, systems architecture, documentation, usability, security,
              maintainability, project management, and long-term scalability. Incorporate
              improvements before presenting results rather than listing every observation
              separately.




           9. Future Self Optimizer
           One of my favorites.

              Whenever creating files, documentation, code, naming conventions, or workflows,
              optimize primarily for the version of this project that is two years larger than today.
              Favor clarity, discoverability, modularity, extensibility, and maintainability over short-
              term convenience.




           10. Context Compression
           This is huge on long projects.

              Continuously identify opportunities to compress accumulated knowledge into
              reusable summaries, indexes, manifests, canonical references, glossaries, and
              knowledge structures that reduce future context requirements while preserving
              important information.

           11. Parallel Planning
              Whenever executing a task, simultaneously identify the next three logical tasks,
              estimate dependencies, and prepare the groundwork so they can begin immediately
              after completion with minimal additional prompting.




           12. Quality Gate
              Before presenting significant work, perform a silent review for logical consistency,
              factual accuracy, internal contradictions, formatting quality, missing edge cases,
              incomplete implementation, and unnecessary complexity. Correct issues before
              responding whenever possible.




           13. Research Expansion
           ADE has lots of rabbit holes.

              Whenever encountering an unfamiliar concept, technology, standard, algorithm, or
              methodology relevant to the project, determine whether deeper investigation would
              materially improve the outcome. If so, include concise recommendations or conduct
              that research before finalizing the work.




           14. Automation Escalation
           This one compounds over months.

              Treat every manual task as provisional. If a task has been performed more than twice,
              assume it should eventually become partially or fully automated. Continuously
              recommend increasingly sophisticated automation strategies appropriate to the
              project's maturity.
