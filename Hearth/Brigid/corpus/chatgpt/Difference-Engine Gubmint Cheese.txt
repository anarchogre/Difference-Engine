Yes, the "How would you like ChatGPT to respond?" box—known as Custom Instructions—really works because it applies permanent system-level prompts to every new chat. 

It alters style, tone, and formatting automatically without needing reminders in individual messages.

Why It Works

System Prompt Injection: 

Your text feeds directly into the model's background instructions before it reads your actual prompt.

Persistent Context: 

It saves you from repeating rules like "use simple words" or "keep answers short" in every single chat.

Global Scope: 

It applies across sessions until you change or turn it off.

What It Can Do

Formatting Rules: 

Force bullet points, specific code languages, or a set response length.

Tone Adjustment: 

Set a formal, casual, or direct speaking style.

Role Alignment: 

Tell the AI your profession or grade level so it matches your knowledge base.

Behavioral Boundaries: 

Stop the AI from lecturing, preaching, or using filler phrases.

Limitations

Instruction Overload: 

Too many conflicting rules can confuse the model or cause it to ignore some constraints.

Stronger Prompts Override It: 

A very specific prompt in your current chat can override your global custom instructions.Not Memory: It is a static rulebook, not a dynamic memory that learns from your daily chats.

Using Projects in ChatGPT allows you to isolate workflows by grouping related chats, custom instructions, and reference files into a single dedicated workspace. This stops you from having to repeatedly upload documents or restate context for ongoing tasks.

How to Set Up and Use Projects

Create a Project: 

Look for the Projects section in your left sidebar, click the + or New Project button, and give your workspace a clear title (e.g., "Q3 Marketing Launch" or "Client Alpha").

Add Custom Instructions: 

Set specific guidelines, preferred tones, or role definitions (such as "Act as a senior copywriter") that automatically apply to every chat inside that project.

Upload Project Files: 

Attach relevant PDFs, spreadsheets, or images so ChatGPT can reference them in real-time during your discussions.

Group Existing Chats: 

Move past conversations into your project by dragging and dropping them, or by clicking the three dots (...) next to a chat title and selecting Move to project.

Start New Work: Initiate fresh conversations directly from inside the active project workspace so they instantly inherit the designated files and custom settings.

Core Governance and Persona Architectures

1. The Human-Governs-the-Loop Cycle

Instead of a reactive human check at the end of a process, governance is shifted upstream.

Definition Phase: 

Authority figures establish explicit decision logic, ethical boundaries, and rights of appeal.

Execution Boundary: 

The GPT persona operates entirely within that locked perimeter.

Audit Signal: 

Deviations or edge cases feed back into modifying the foundational instructions rather than just editing individual outputs.

2. Fully Automated vs. Human-First Model Spec Loops

OpenAI and enterprise systems manage behavioral drift using continuous specification loops:

Automated Adjustment Loop: 

A high-capability reasoning model analyzes areas where previous outputs or user rankings conflicted with safety or performance criteria, drafts a revised plain-text rule, and tests it against benchmark sets.

Human-First Validation Loop: 

Researchers or compliance managers author rule updates, which the secondary validator model tests against original user justifications to prevent internal contradiction.

3. Open-Environment Persona Evolution Loops

Maintaining long-term identity continuity without losing alignment requires multi-timescale loops:

State Tracking: 

Logging contextual shifts across sessions while stripping transient noise.

Internal Persona Mapping: 

Monitoring internal activation spaces (via techniques like sparse autoencoders) to detect the unintended triggering of negative or misaligned traits (such as sycophancy or a toxic persona feature).

Benign Re-balancing: 

Re-injecting small sets of clean, pro-social instructional data to reverse accidental behavioral drift during active fine-tuning.

To stop ChatGPT and GPT models from forgetting rules or drifting off track, use system prompts with rigid negative constraints, enforce structured JSON or XML output formats, place critical rules at the very end of your prompt, and use repeated reminder tokens in multi-turn chats.

Core Setup Methods

System Prompt Priority: 

Put your most important rules at the very top and repeat the most critical constraints at the very bottom of your instructions.

Negative Constraints: 

State exact prohibitions clearly (e.g., "Do not use bullet points," "Never mention competitor names") rather than vague advice.

Structured Outputs: 

Force JSON or specific XML tags so the model breaks if it deviates from the required format.

Few-Shot Examples: 

Give the model 2 or 3 examples of correct behavior versus wrong behavior to anchor its responses.

Active Drift Prevention

Regular Resets: 

Start a fresh chat session or clear context windows after long conversational threads begin to degrade.

Validation Prompts: 

Add a hidden checking step where the model reviews its own draft against your rules before outputting the final answer.

Temperature Adjustment: 

Lower the temperature setting to 0 or 0.2 to make the model stick strictly to predictable, literal instructions.
