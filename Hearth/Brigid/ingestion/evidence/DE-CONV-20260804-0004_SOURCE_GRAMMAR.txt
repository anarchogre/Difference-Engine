Yes, the "How would you like ChatGPT to respond?" box—known as Custom Instructions—really works because it applies permanent system-level prompts to every new chat. 

It alters style, tone, and formatting automatically without needing reminders in individual messages.

Why It Works

System Prompt Injection: 

Your text feeds directly into the model's background instructions before it reads your actual prompt.

Persistent Context: 

It saves you from repeating rules like "use simple words" or "keep answers short" in every single chat.

Global Scope: 

It applies across sessions until you change or turn it off.

What It Can Do

Formatting Rules: 

Force bullet points, specific code languages, or a set response length.

Tone Adjustment: 

Set a formal, casual, or direct speaking style.

Role Alignment: 

Tell the AI your profession or grade level so it matches your knowledge base.

Behavioral Boundaries: 

Stop the AI from lecturing, preaching, or using filler phrases.

Limitations

Instruction Overload: 

Too many conflicting rules can confuse the model or cause it to ignore some constraints.

Stronger Prompts Override It: 

A very specific prompt in your current chat can override your global custom instructions.Not Memory: It is a static rulebook, not a dynamic memory that learns from your daily chats.

To get pure workflow from ChatGPT, use a strict system prompt or opening directive that bans conversational filler, explanations, and pleasantries. 

Demand a numbered step-by-step list containing only actions, inputs, and outputs.

Best Directives to Use

The "Zero Fluff" Prompt: 

Give me a pure workflow. No intro. No outro. No explanations. Only provide a numbered step-by-step list of actions, inputs, and outputs.

The "Strict Execution" Prompt: 

Act as a process engine. Output only the raw workflow steps. Omit all conversational text, context, or summaries."

Key Rules to Add

Ban text: 

Tell ChatGPT to skip "Here is your workflow" and "Let me know if you need help."

Format strictly: 

Ask for a strict format like 

Step -> Action -> Output.

Cut length: Add "Keep each step under 10 words" if you want it very short.

Using Projects in ChatGPT allows you to isolate workflows by grouping related chats, custom instructions, and reference files into a single dedicated workspace. This stops you from having to repeatedly upload documents or restate context for ongoing tasks.

How to Set Up and Use Projects

Create a Project: 

Look for the Projects section in your left sidebar, click the + or New Project button, and give your workspace a clear title (e.g., "Q3 Marketing Launch" or "Client Alpha").

Add Custom Instructions: 

Set specific guidelines, preferred tones, or role definitions (such as "Act as a senior copywriter") that automatically apply to every chat inside that project.

Upload Project Files: 

Attach relevant PDFs, spreadsheets, or images so ChatGPT can reference them in real-time during your discussions.

Group Existing Chats: 

Move past conversations into your project by dragging and dropping them, or by clicking the three dots (...) next to a chat title and selecting Move to project.

Start New Work: Initiate fresh conversations directly from inside the active project workspace so they instantly inherit the designated files and custom settings.

Benefits of Isolating Workflows

Persistent Context: 

Maintain a continuous background layer so the AI remembers prior decisions without reading your entire unrelated chat history.

Reduced Clutter: 

Clear out your main sidebar by sorting long-running, multi-part assignments into dedicated operational hubs.

Scoped Memory: 

Prevent context from bleeding across unrelated topics by keeping sensitive or specialized tasks strictly walled off.

Introduce Deterministic Validators: 

Constrain agentic workflows so state mutations require explicit programmatic validation rather than open-ended model generation.
