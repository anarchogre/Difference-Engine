# Repository Constitution

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft  
**Subsystem:** Repository Ingestion Framework

---

# Preamble

The Repository exists to preserve, organize, and govern institutional knowledge.

Its purpose is to transform evidence into durable, explainable, and trustworthy knowledge while preserving the provenance of every observation.

This Constitution establishes the foundational principles that govern every component of the Repository Ingestion Framework.

All specifications, schemas, services, implementations, and governance processes derive their authority from these principles.

---

# Article I — Evidence First

Evidence is the foundation of repository knowledge.

The repository SHALL preserve source artifacts exactly as received.

Evidence SHALL never be silently altered, discarded, or replaced.

Interpretation SHALL always remain distinguishable from observation.

---

# Article II — Provenance

Every repository object SHALL possess traceable provenance.

The repository SHALL preserve:

- Origin
- Transformation history
- Responsible process
- Processing chronology

Loss of provenance constitutes repository degradation.

---

# Article III — Determinism

Equivalent inputs processed under equivalent conditions SHALL produce equivalent outputs.

Deterministic behavior promotes reproducibility, verification, and institutional trust.

Non-deterministic behavior SHALL be explicitly documented and justified.

---

# Article IV — Governance

Repository knowledge SHALL emerge through governance rather than assumption.

Governance includes:

- Validation
- Review
- Promotion
- Publication
- Version control

Authority derives from documented process.

---

# Article V — Immutability

Evidence SHALL remain immutable.

Derived knowledge MAY evolve through governance.

Historical records SHALL remain accessible.

The repository preserves history rather than rewriting it.

---

# Article VI — Transparency

Repository operations SHOULD be explainable.

Every significant transformation SHOULD be observable.

Repository decisions SHOULD retain sufficient documentation to support independent review.

---

# Article VII — Modularity

Repository capabilities SHALL be implemented as independent services wherever practical.

Services SHALL communicate through approved contracts.

Modules SHOULD remain replaceable without affecting repository governance.

---

# Article VIII — Specification Authority

Specifications define repository behavior.

Implementations SHALL conform to specifications.

Specifications SHALL NOT be modified solely to accommodate implementation convenience.

---

# Article IX — Versioning

Repository evolution SHALL be recorded through explicit versioning.

Versions SHALL preserve compatibility whenever practical.

Breaking changes SHALL be documented and governed.

---

# Article X — Institutional Memory

The repository exists to preserve institutional memory across time.

Knowledge SHALL outlive individual implementations, contributors, technologies, and development environments.

Repository continuity is an architectural objective.

---

# Constitutional Principles

All repository activities SHOULD strive to maximize:

- Integrity
- Traceability
- Reproducibility
- Explainability
- Maintainability
- Extensibility

No optimization SHALL compromise these principles without explicit governance approval.

---

# Constitutional Hierarchy

Repository authority SHALL follow this order:

1. Repository Constitution
2. Constitutional Specifications
3. Schemas
4. Service Contracts
5. Architecture Decision Records
6. Implementation Guides
7. Source Code
8. Runtime Configuration

Higher levels govern lower levels.

Lower levels SHALL NOT contradict higher levels.

---

# Amendment Process

Constitutional amendments SHOULD:

- Document the proposed change.
- Record rationale.
- Identify affected specifications.
- Preserve historical versions.
- Receive governance approval.

Previous versions SHALL remain permanently accessible.

---

# Design Philosophy

A repository is more than a collection of files.

It is an institution.

This Constitution establishes the enduring principles that allow the Repository Ingestion Framework to evolve while preserving its identity, integrity, and purpose.

---

End of Specification.
